Learning Approach

Preparation proceeds in layers, each resting on the one beneath it. It began with mathematics — linear algebra, calculus, and probability — studied before any machine learning. Deep learning came next, through Dive into Deep Learning, followed by a second, slower pass through neural network fundamentals, drawing mainly on Michael Nielsen's Neural Networks and Deep Learning, in which each result is derived again and then implemented. Throughout, the mathematics is treated as mathematics: definitions, statements, and derivations are studied on their own terms, not only as tools for the code.

The working standard is that a concept counts as understood only once it can be stated precisely and implemented by hand. Each notebook section states the key equations, implements them from scratch in PyTorch, and checks the result against an independent reference — hand-written backpropagation against autograd, for example. Abstractions are introduced only after the mechanism beneath them has been built: raw tensors first, then manual gradients, then nn.Module.

Where existing tools fall short, small ones are built or adapted along the way — for example KaoNotes, a LaTeX template adapted for mathematical notes.

The research stage is driven by the literature. Reading starts from the original KAN paper and extends to related theoretical work; unfamiliar concepts are studied as they appear — in depth where they are central, and only as far as needed where they are peripheral.

Primary references, drawn on as needed: MIT 18.06 Linear Algebra; Thomas' Calculus / MIT 18.01–18.02; MIT 6.041/18.05 Probability; Dive into Deep Learning (Zhang, Lipton, Li & Smola; an open textbook used in university courses, including at Stanford); and Michael Nielsen's Neural Networks and Deep Learning.

This preparation is kept in a separate repository: notebooks pairing each formula with a self-written implementation, together with notes on concepts met in the literature. It precedes the KAN project and is not counted as part of its output.
